How Your Group Investment Account Works

6 min read Saving & Investing

If your Life Ready group plan includes a group investment account — often structured as a Group RRSP — it's one of the more valuable pieces of your compensation, even though it doesn't show up as cash in your paycheque. Here's what's actually happening behind the scenes.

What "group" means

A group plan pools employees together to access institutional-style investment options and typically lower fees than an individual investor could get on their own. Life Ready sets up the plan; you and, in many cases, your employer contribute into your own individual account within it.

Why employer contributions matter

If Life Ready matches some or all of your contributions, that match is effectively part of your compensation. Not contributing enough to get the full match is often described as leaving free money on the table — it's worth checking your plan details to see if a match applies and what percentage gets you the maximum.

Pre-tax contributions and your paycheque

RRSP-type contributions typically reduce your taxable income in the year you contribute, which can lower the income tax withheld from your pay. The money then grows tax-deferred until you withdraw it, usually in retirement when your income — and often your tax rate — may be lower.

How compounding works in your favor

Investment growth earns returns not just on what you put in, but on the returns you've already earned. The earlier you start, the more time compounding has to work — which is why even small, consistent contributions in your 20s and 30s can outperform larger contributions started later.

Choosing your investments

Most group plans offer a lineup of funds ranging from conservative (more bonds, less volatility) to growth-focused (more equities, more volatility, more long-term growth potential). Many plans also offer target-date funds that automatically shift toward conservative investments as you approach a chosen retirement year.
