Understanding Your Group Insurance Coverage

5 min read Insurance

Group insurance is often one of the least understood parts of a benefits package, mostly because it's easy to ignore until you actually need it. Here's what the main pieces usually cover, in plain language.

Extended health care

This typically tops up what your provincial health plan doesn't cover — things like prescription drugs, physiotherapy, massage therapy, mental health counselling, vision care, and medical equipment. Most plans have annual maximums per category, so it's worth knowing your limits before booking an expensive treatment.

Dental care

Usually split into tiers: basic care (cleanings, exams, fillings), major restorative care (crowns, bridges), and sometimes orthodontics. Preventive care is often covered at a higher percentage than major work, which is part of why regular checkups are encouraged.

Life and disability coverage

Group life insurance typically pays a lump sum to your named beneficiary if you pass away while covered — make sure this is up to date, especially after a major life change like marriage or having a child. Disability coverage (short-term and/or long-term) replaces a portion of your income if you can't work due to illness or injury.

How claims usually work

  1. Pay for the eligible expense (or have it billed directly if your provider offers direct billing)
  2. Submit your claim through the insurer's portal or app, along with receipts
  3. The insurer processes the claim against your plan's coverage percentages and annual maximums
  4. Reimbursement is typically deposited directly to your bank account within a few business days

Before a big expense

For anything significant — like orthodontic work or a major dental procedure — it's worth asking your provider for a pre-determination, which tells you in advance what will actually be covered.
